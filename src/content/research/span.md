Adolescence is a sensitive period of brain maturation during which exposure to substances and the emergence of psychopathology can have lasting consequences. The SPAN group uses large-scale longitudinal neuroimaging cohorts—including the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) Study, IMAGEN, and ENIGMA-Addiction—to characterize how alcohol, cannabis, nicotine, and other substances affect developing brain structure and function. We also examine how internalizing and externalizing psychopathology co-occur with substance use and how neurodevelopmental trajectories from childhood through young adulthood moderate risk and resilience.
